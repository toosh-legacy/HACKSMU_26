Research context

"When an elephant rumble overlaps in both time and frequency with noise sources, it becomes difficult or impossible to measure the acoustic properties of the call without influence from the noise source, rendering these recordings useless for analysis."

— Dr. Mickey Pardo, ElephantVoices

ElephantVoices has been recording and cataloguing elephant calls since the 1980s. Decades of field work in Kenya and Mozambique have produced thousands of recordings — many of which are partially or entirely unusable because of mechanical noise that arrived in frame. Airplanes, generators, and passing vehicles overlap the infrasound band where elephants communicate.

Tribal processes those recordings. It identifies the noise type, separates the signal from the interference, and returns a cleaned WAV alongside acoustic measurements that can actually be trusted.

Why it's hard

Frequency overlap

Generator harmonics and airplane noise land directly in the 10–1,000 Hz band where elephant calls live. Simple high-pass filters remove the signal along with the noise.

No clean reference

Field recordings rarely include a noise-only segment captured in the same conditions. Standard spectral subtraction requires one. NMF-based separation doesn't.

Simultaneous callers

Multiple elephants often rumble at the same time. Their calls overlap in frequency, making it hard to isolate individual F0 tracks without a second decomposition pass.

Scale of the archive

Decades of recordings — too many to clean by hand. Any solution has to run unattended across hundreds of files and flag its own uncertainty.

Who it's for

Field biologists

Drop a WAV file and get a cleaned version back with acoustic measurements. No signal processing expertise required.

Acoustic researchers

Access per-call F0 estimates, harmonic completeness scores, and SNR deltas. Cluster views surface recurring call patterns across the dataset.

Conservation teams

Behavioral hypotheses generated from the cluster structure give non-specialists a starting point for interpreting what the data might mean.

What you get

Cleaned WAV files

Noise removed via NMF soft-mask. Original dynamic range preserved. Harmonic structure reconstructed where the noise obscured it.

Separated callers

Dual-F0 detection identifies simultaneous calls. Each caller gets their own output track when the algorithm finds two distinct fundamental frequencies.

Behavioural clusters

UMAP projection followed by K-means groups calls by acoustic similarity. Each cluster gets a label, a summary, and a similarity graph across herds.

AI hypotheses

Gemini reads the cluster summaries and returns plain-language hypotheses about what each group of calls might represent behaviourally.
